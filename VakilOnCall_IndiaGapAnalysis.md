## **3. India-Specific Gaps — What Attorney Shield Does NOT Address** 

These are scenarios and legal realities unique to India that Attorney Shield's model does not cover and that your app must be specifically designed to handle. 

## **3.1 The Bribery Encounter — India's Most Common Police Interaction** 

In the US, police bribery at checkpoints is rare and prosecuted. In India, it is a normalized daily reality. Traffic police stopping vehicles for 'document checks' is widely understood to be bribery collection — not law enforcement. The Karnataka DGP's directive banning such stops has been openly and routinely ignored. Commuters report being targeted specifically for not speaking the local language. 

## **What the App Needs for This Scenario** 

- A 'Traffic Stop' mode: displays what documents police can legally demand (RC, DL, insurance, PUC) — and what they cannot demand 

- Lawyer on call coaches user: 'You do not have to pay. Ask for a challan receipt. If they refuse to give a receipt, they cannot legally collect.' 

- Record the interaction (with user's consent displayed prominently on screen) 

- One-tap SOS to emergency contacts with GPS location — identical to Attorney Shield but critically needed here too 

- Post-encounter: lawyer can advise on filing complaint with senior police officer or online portal 

## **3.2 The FIR Refusal — A Uniquely Indian Problem** 

The Supreme Court in Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of U.P. (2014) made it mandatory for police to register an FIR for any cognizable offence. Despite this, police routinely refuse to register FIRs — especially when the accused is politically connected, socially dominant, or when the victim is from a marginalised community. This has no equivalent in the US legal context. 

## **What the App Needs for This Scenario** 

- Lawyer explains the Lalita Kumari ruling: police refusal to register FIR is illegal and contempt of court 

- Step-by-step guidance: (1) Request FIR, (2) If refused, approach SP under Section 154(3) BNSS, (3) File complaint online via PGPortal or state police website, (4) Approach magistrate directly under Section 175 BNSS 

- e-FIR guidance: BNSS now allows online FIR filing for certain offences — app lawyer must know which ones 

- Document refusal: note officer name, badge number, time — this becomes evidence for a contempt petition 

## **3.3 Custodial Situations — Far More Dangerous in India** 

In the US, Attorney Shield's main use case is a traffic stop where a lawyer monitors via video. In India, the stakes are exponentially higher. With 2,739 custodial deaths in 2024 alone, and zero convictions for any of them between 2018-2022, being taken into Indian police custody without a lawyer is a genuinely life-threatening situation for many citizens — particularly from Dalit, minority, and migrant communities. 

## **What the App Needs for This Scenario** 

• Immediate SOS: when user activates 'I am being arrested' mode, automatically notifies 3 emergency contacts with GPS, timestamp, officer details if known 

• DK Basu Rights Checklist displayed to user AND readable to officer: right to arrest memo, right to inform family, right to medical examination, right to lawyer 

- Lawyer stays on call and instructs user to verbally invoke rights on record 

• If phone is confiscated — a real risk — the SOS has already fired. Family knows. This alone could save lives. 

• Post-custody: lawyer guides family on filing habeas corpus petition at High Court if detention is illegal 

- NHRC complaint filing guidance if DK Basu guidelines are violated 

## **3.4 The New Criminal Laws (BNS/BNSS/BSA) — A Unique Transitional Gap** 

Since July 1, 2024, India has operated under entirely new criminal laws. Cases before July 2024 follow IPC/CrPC. Cases after follow BNS/BNSS. Even police officers and lawyers are confused. Citizens are completely unaware. This creates a specific gap that no other app addresses. 

- The BNSS extended police detention to 60-90 days — citizens don't know this changed 

- New e-FIR provisions exist but almost no one uses them — app can bridge this 

- Section numbers have changed — a lawyer citing old IPC sections may be citing wrong law 

- Fresh law graduates who graduated post-2024 may know BNS better than seniors — this is actually your competitive advantage in lawyer quality 

## **3.5 Caste, Religion, and Identity-Based Targeting** 

This has no US equivalent at the same systemic scale. India's police force has documented patterns of targeting Dalits, Muslims, Adivasis, and migrant workers. The Global Torture Index 2025 specifically flagged that 'human rights defenders, particularly those addressing land and environmental issues, frequently face preventive detention, torture and illegal arrests.' The app cannot ignore this dimension. 

## **Sensitive Design Considerations** 

- Lawyer on call must be trained in identity-based rights: SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act protections, minority rights, migrant worker rights under different state laws 

- Consider a specific 'I am being targeted based on identity' mode with different legal guidance 

- Language matching: a Dalit farmer in UP stopped by police needs a Hindi-speaking lawyer who knows UP local laws — not a generic English consultation 

- Data: track patterns of encounter types and locations — anonymized data could reveal systemic targeting 

## **3.6 The Digital Arrest Fraud — A New India-Specific Threat** 

India has a new and rapidly growing threat: scammers impersonating police officers, CBI officials, and judges via phone or video call, threatening 'digital arrest' and demanding money. Courts have begun treating this seriously — the Kolkata Sessions Court handed life imprisonment to the mastermind of a pan-India digital arrest racket in 2024. Your app can also serve as a verification tool here. 

## **Opportunity: Anti-Digital-Arrest Feature** 

- Users can consult a lawyer: 'Is this a real police call or a scam?' — lawyers can instantly identify red flags 

- In-app information: 'Real police NEVER conduct digital arrests. No legitimate officer asks for money over phone.' 

- This feature costs nothing to add and could go viral — it's a widely reported problem with high emotional resonance 

- Positions app not just as encounter help but as general legal safety tool — broader TAM

## **The Golden Rule for Misuse Prevention** 

• Every call is recorded, timestamped, and GPS-tagged. This single feature deters the majority of misuse from both sides. 

• Lawyers who give bad advice can be identified and removed. Users who misuse can be banned. 

• Make this feature visible and prominent — it protects you legally and builds trust with users and regulators. 

• Store recordings for minimum 90 days. Make them accessible to NHRC, courts, and Bar Council on receipt of proper legal notice.

## **6. The BNS/BNSS/BSA Changes — What Your App Must Know**

|**Area**|**Old Law (IPC/CrPC)**|**New Law (BNS/BNSS)**|**Impact on App**|

## **7. Attorney Shield — What to Borrow vs What to Ignore** 

## **BORROW — These translate well** 

• One-tap instant connection to lawyer — the core UX must be this fast 

• Auto-recording of all calls with secure storage — legally and ethically essential in India 

• Emergency SOS with GPS to trusted contacts — even more critical in India given phone confiscation risk 

• Document storage (adapt for Indian docs: Aadhaar, RC, DL, PUC, insurance) 

• PIN to end session — user controls when encounter ends, not police 

• Video call option — seeing the officer on screen is deterrent and evidence 

• Post-encounter follow-up flow 

• Rating and review of lawyers after call 

## **IGNORE or REDESIGN — US-specific, won't work in India** 

- Senior lawyer network (16yr avg exp) — too expensive, use fresh graduates 

- Subscription model — token model is better for India's payment psychology 

- Nationwide uniform legal framework assumption — India has state-level law variations 

- English-only interface — fatal flaw for India 

- Member Protection Warranty (reimburse fines) — regulatory complexity in India, skip for now 

- US-style Miranda rights information — must be replaced with DK Basu / BNSS rights 

- Traffic stop only focus — India needs FIR refusal, custodial, bribery scenarios too 

- No scenario pre-selection — India needs scenario routing for call quality 

## **8. Summary — The India Opportunity in One Paragraph** 

India's police-citizen legal gap is not just larger than America's — it is structurally different in nature. The US problem is primarily about knowing your rights. India's problem is about having no practical recourse even when you know your rights. Police routinely ignore Supreme Court directives with zero consequences. Custodial deaths number in the thousands with zero prosecutions. Petty bribery is estimated at nearly Rs 4,000 crore annually. New criminal laws since July 2024 have created a dual legal system that even trained lawyers are confused by. And for the first time, a real-time lawyer connection — changing the power dynamic of a police encounter before it escalates — is technically and economically feasible through a mobile app. No existing Indian legal tech platform has built this. Attorney Shield proved the model works in the US. Your job is to build it for a country where it is needed ten times more urgently. 

## **Three Things That Make Your India App Non-Negotiable** 

- Auto-recording + SOS: fires the moment encounter starts, before phone can be confiscated. This one feature alone changes the power dynamic. 

- Scenario-specific guidance: India has encounter types (bribe, FIR refusal, custodial arrest, digital scam) that Attorney Shield was never designed for. 

- Fresh lawyer supply + language routing: makes 24/7 availability actually possible, unlike every competitor who uses expensive senior lawyers who don't pick up at 2am.
